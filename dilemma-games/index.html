The dictator's dilemma: two games

POLI6015, Week 1. In class, one projector screen is run by the seminar leader and students play on their phones. Either game can also be played alone against the computer. Draft for Jamie: not yet used with students.

The Harvest Report

About 30 minutes · one phone per student · 6 or more players

Every student is a numbered village with a secret harvest. They report it to the Chairman, first with nothing at stake, then under a quota that punishes the lowest reports. What happens when the state believes the numbers?

Wintrobe (1998), ch. 1: fear makes reports false (p. 22); the enforcement problem (pp. 25–26); competing agencies (pp. 34–35).

Run it in class: projector screen Join on a phone Play against the computer

The Petition Box

About 35 minutes · one phone per team, plus one for the Police Chief

The Palace rules Valmora, a country of four provinces, each with a hidden grievance. Each year it spends 8 tokens on aid, police and petition boxes, and the provinces praise, stay silent, petition or protest. Does the Palace dare to open the box?

Wintrobe (1998), ch. 1: the paradox and the subject's dilemma (p. 22); repression and loyalty (p. 33); the security forces as the main threat (p. 34); rents (p. 36); power traded for security (p. 38).

Run it in class: projector screen Join on a phone Play against the computer
Running it in class

Open the projector screen on the leader's laptop. Students go to the address it shows and type the four-letter code. Playing against the computer needs no code: it runs on one device and sends nothing online.
